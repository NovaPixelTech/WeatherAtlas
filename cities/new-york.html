Seasons in New York

New York is warmest in July, when average highs reach 29.5°C, and coolest in January at 4.4°C. Rainfall peaks in July (139.3 mm) and is lowest in November (76.2 mm).

May, June, September, October offer the closest match to the typical 18–28°C comfort range with moderate rain. These figures are ten-year monthly averages from Open-Meteo, so individual years can differ.

Best time to visit New York

Based on long-term averages, May is usually the most comfortable month, with an average high of 21.1°C and about 94.2 mm of precipitation. The hottest month is July and the coolest is January; the driest month is November and the wettest is July.

How New York compares

With an annual mean of 12.6°C and about 1298.9 mm of rain, New York has moderate climate and a fairly wet yearly rainfall profile. Travellers who prefer warm, dry weather should focus on May, while those who do not mind rain can visit in July.

Frequently asked questions

What is the climate like in New York?

Over the last ten years New York averaged a daytime high of 17°C and a nighttime low of 8.1°C, with about 1298.9 mm of precipitation per year. The warmest month on average was July and the coldest was January.

When is the best time of year to visit New York?

The driest month is November and the wettest is July. The hottest average high is July at 29.5°C, and the coolest is January at 4.4°C, so the milder months are usually the most comfortable for outdoor plans.

Does it snow in New York?

Snow is possible in New York: the coldest average nights drop to around -3.6°C, which is at or below freezing. Frost and occasional snow can occur in the coldest months.
