How to read a climate chart (and pick the best month to travel)

Every WeatherOrbit city guide includes a monthly climate chart with four columns: average high, average low, average, and precipitation. Here's how to use them to plan a trip.

1. Start with the highs and lows

The average high tells you how warm it gets during the day; the average low tells you how cool it gets at night. A 15°C gap between high and low (common in deserts) means pack layers. A small gap (common in tropics) means consistent warmth.

2. Check precipitation, not just temperature

A month can be warm but rainy. Look at the precipitation column: under 50 mm is dry, 50–150 mm is moderate, over 150 mm is wet. The driest month on each guide is highlighted in the stats.

3. Find the "comfort window"

Most travellers prefer highs between 18°C and 28°C with low precipitation. The "Best time to visit" section on each city guide picks the month with the lowest precipitation among the comfortable ones.

4. Watch for extremes

The hottest and coldest months are shown in the stats. If you're sensitive to heat or cold, avoid those. Coastal cities often have milder extremes than inland ones at the same latitude.
